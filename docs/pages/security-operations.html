OPNSENSE / MONITORING / RECOVERY

Keeping OPNsense Running

A firewall can look fine after an update and still have a broken background service or a stale routing state. I have spent almost as much time checking what survives a reboot as I have configuring the features themselves.

Updates and persistent configuration

My post-update checks cover internet routing, DHCP and DNS, IPv6 delegation, FreeRADIUS and certificate revocation, the guest portal, LLDP, WireGuard, Suricata and Zenarmor. I compare service status with actual client behavior or gateway state where practical.

A standard OPNsense configuration backup doesn't contain every custom hook, cron job or NAS-side dependency I use. I keep a separate recovery list for RADIUS and CRL updates, LLDP settings, dynamic portal records and other local automation.

The 6 November OPNsense update

I updated the main gateway to OPNsense 26.7.8_2 and checked it after reboot. The custom service hooks and scheduled jobs survived, including my FreeRADIUS/CRL work, WAN IPv6 recovery, portal updates and LLDP settings. I checked the actual running services and the relevant saved configuration rather than trusting the version number alone.

My Vodafone-to-LTE-to-Vodafone failover, including VPN recovery, had passed a separate real test on 5 November. I did not repeat that forced failover on 6 November, so I have kept those two observations apart.

The update also reminded me why I keep post-boot notes: the familiar invalid PF gateway states appeared again. I requested targeted cleanup, but the startup cause still needs investigating. Zenarmor recorded UDP flow warnings without a confirmed client outage, so I haven't changed inspection settings merely to silence the logs.

I corrected overly broad file permissions in the UPS monitoring configuration. They read correctly afterward, but I still need to see whether that survives the next normal settings regeneration. LLDP also showed my own device as a neighbor alongside the real switch; that observation alone was not enough to claim a physical loop.

When the LTE modem did not return after reboot

While testing the new IPv6-only VLAN on 9 November, the secondary LTE modem did not bring its PPP uplink back after the gateway restarted. I inspected the device and tried a manual USB reset and interface reconfiguration. The link still had no usable address until a subsequent device reset; the modem then recovered in the observed session.

I haven't identified why the first attempt failed, added automatic USB recovery or repeated the full primary-to-LTE failover sequence after that incident. It would be misleading to call this a permanent fix. The short incident note →

Monitoring and resilience

I use service health checks and notification routing on the gateway, and collect metrics for the wider infrastructure. I also tested the backup internet path on 5 November: primary WAN to LTE and back, including VPN recovery. That doesn't prove the behavior of every service, so more selective failover tests remain on my list.

Power protection has similar limits: I tested UPS communication and a controlled loss of mains power, but not every connected device has been confirmed to receive battery-backed power.

What remains open

The startup cause of the invalid PF states is still unresolved. I also want more complete tests following WAN reconnects, prefix changes, UPS events and gateway restarts, with the resulting observations added to the working notes.
