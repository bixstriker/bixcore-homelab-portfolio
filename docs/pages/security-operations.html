OPNSENSE / MONITORING / RECOVERY

Keeping OPNsense Running

A firewall can look fine after an update and still have a broken background service or a stale routing state. I have spent almost as much time checking what survives a reboot as I have configuring the features themselves.

Updates and persistent configuration

My post-update checks cover internet routing, DHCP and DNS, IPv6 delegation, FreeRADIUS and certificate revocation, the guest portal, LLDP, WireGuard, Suricata and Zenarmor. I compare service status with actual client behavior or gateway state where practical.

A standard OPNsense configuration backup doesn't contain every custom hook, cron job or NAS-side dependency I use. I keep a separate recovery list for RADIUS and CRL updates, LLDP settings, dynamic portal records and other local automation.

The 6 November OPNsense update

I updated the main gateway to OPNsense 26.7.8_2 and checked it after reboot. The custom service hooks and scheduled jobs survived, including my FreeRADIUS/CRL work, WAN IPv6 recovery, portal updates and LLDP settings. I checked the actual running services and the relevant saved configuration rather than trusting the version number alone.

My Vodafone-to-LTE-to-Vodafone failover, including VPN recovery, had passed a separate real test on 5 November. I did not repeat that forced failover on 6 November, so I have kept those two observations apart.

The update also reminded me why I keep post-boot notes: the familiar invalid PF gateway states appeared again. I requested targeted cleanup, but the startup cause still needs investigating. Zenarmor recorded UDP flow warnings without a confirmed client outage, so I haven't changed inspection settings merely to silence the logs.

I corrected overly broad file permissions in the UPS monitoring configuration. At that earlier checkpoint I had not yet checked regeneration; the later November tests below confirmed the persistent correction. LLDP also showed my own device as a neighbor alongside the real switch; that observation alone was not enough to claim a physical loop.

Historical incident: the old USB LTE modem after reboot

While testing the new IPv6-only VLAN on 9 November, the former USB LTE modem did not bring its PPP uplink back after the gateway restarted. I inspected the device and tried a manual USB reset and interface reconfiguration. The link still had no usable address until a subsequent device reset; the modem then recovered in the observed session.

I did not establish the cause of the old USB incident. I later retired that modem and switched to Ethernet LTE. The previously tested full primary-to-LTE failover and VPN recovery are separate from that one boot incident. The short incident note →

Why I moved remote Plex streaming to Vodafone

I compared actual remote downloads through Windscribe and the regular WAN. The measured VPN sessions were around 2.0–3.4 MB/s; Vodafone gave 4.4–5.1 MB/s. After moving ordinary playback, I checked remote 4K Direct Play on a phone at roughly 29 Mbit/s. These are application measurements, not a bandwidth guarantee.

I retained a dedicated Suricata IPS path for incoming Plex traffic and verified a controlled test signature was actually dropped. I haven't independently measured every video packet.

Windscribe keeps its own forwarded endpoint for the NAS watchdog and the established HomeWG backup path. I had previously tested the real WAN-to-LTE failover and VPN/WireGuard recovery; Plex's new outbound policy did not replace that design.

Monitoring and resilience

I use service health checks and notification routing on the gateway, and collect metrics for the wider infrastructure. I also tested the backup internet path on 5 November: primary WAN to LTE and back, including VPN recovery. This verified the LTE/VPN recovery path. I continue checking individual service behavior when the network changes.

Power protection has similar limits: I tested UPS communication and a controlled loss of mains power, but not every connected device has been confirmed to receive battery-backed power.

What changed on 26–27 November

I replaced the earlier USB LTE modem with an Ethernet-connected Zyxel and tested the new Vodafone-to-LTE-to-Vodafone recovery path physically. The new route observer and targeted PF cleanup recovered Windscribe automatically in both directions. On a separate reboot, the startup-state mitigation removed 25 invalid states. The full failover and recovery project →

My NUT permission correction now survives configuration regeneration and reboot. The wider recovery audit also checked custom hooks, FreeRADIUS, DNS and other running services after offline imaging. The image and custom archive were verified, but a test restore is still outstanding.

What remains open

The startup cause of the invalid PF states is still unresolved. I also want more complete tests following WAN reconnects, prefix changes, UPS events and gateway restarts, with the resulting observations added to the working notes.
