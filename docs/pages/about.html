ABOUT ME

Felix Bistriker

I'm an engineer with a background in mechanical engineering, research and electronic security systems. BiXCore is my personal networking and security lab, where I get to explore infrastructure in more depth and learn by building it myself.

My GitHub profile ↗ BiXCore repository ↗

From engineering projects to networking

My professional work has focused on electronic security systems: fire detection, intrusion alarms, video surveillance and access control. Over more than ten years, I've worked on installation, commissioning, maintenance, troubleshooting and project coordination. I have also managed technical teams and helped bring complex systems into operation.

The networking side is part of my day-to-day professional work, especially in video surveillance. I integrate systems with Active Directory, configure managed switches, firewalls, routers and GSM modems, and work on connectivity and configuration for Axis IP cameras and video servers. Troubleshooting a camera system often means following the problem through network addressing, routing, access rules and server services, not just looking at the video application.

That practical experience made me want to understand more of what happens behind the devices: switching, routing, authentication, certificates and the infrastructure that keeps everything connected.

BiXCore gives me a place to go further with those technologies. I can build a complete setup, deliberately test a failure, check logs and packets, and document both the fix and the things I still need to understand.

What I work with in BiXCore

Networks and security

OPNsense, IPv4/IPv6, VLANs, managed switching, Wi-Fi, WireGuard, Suricata, Zenarmor and FreeRADIUS with EAP-TLS.

Identity and certificates

Windows Server, Active Directory, AD CS, NDES/SCEP, LDAPS, Group Policy and certificate-based client enrollment.

Linux and virtualization

Debian, Kubuntu, Proxmox VE, ZFS, Docker, system services, and virtual firewall testing with Windows and Linux clients.

Monitoring and troubleshooting

Prometheus, Grafana, logs, packet captures, protocol testing and writing recovery procedures based on the behavior I actually observe.

Where I want to take the lab

I'd like to make BiXCore a useful record of how my networking skills develop. I want to explore more commercial firewall platforms, build an isolated Wi-Fi and NAC test environment, go further with IPv6 and recovery testing, and measure how traffic inspection tools such as Zenarmor perform under load.
