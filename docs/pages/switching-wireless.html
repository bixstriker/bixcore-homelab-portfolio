Switching, Wi-Fi and 802.1X

The switching and Wi-Fi side of BiXCore has been a good place to learn how the same network looks from three different directions: the client, the access point or switch, and the RADIUS server.

Switches and VLANs

I use managed Omada and Grandstream switches to separate client, guest, infrastructure and lab traffic. I checked tagged and untagged VLAN membership, port PVIDs and trunk behavior in the actual switch interfaces. Exported configuration data helped with cross-checking, but it did not replace looking at the live settings.

The switches also gave me a chance to examine vendor-specific configuration formats and compare two devices without assuming their ports had identical roles.

Switch administration through TACACS+

I added a separate TACACS+ service for administrative sign-in on my Omada and Barox switches. It uses AD over LDAPS and distinguishes full administrators from restricted accounts. The failure test was especially useful: Barox switched to its local recovery account, while Omada did not do so in the tested TACACS+-then-local setup, even though explicit local login worked. I have reported the behavior to TP-Link, but I don't yet know the cause.

Enterprise Wi-Fi and network access

The Wi-Fi setup uses managed UniFi access points and a locally hosted controller. For supported clients, access is authenticated using EAP-TLS certificates through FreeRADIUS rather than a shared password. Wired 802.1X adds another layer: devices such as domain controllers need careful handling so that network authentication doesn't depend on services they can't reach yet.

I also investigated what could be learned from the controller backup format. Those investigations helped me understand the stored WLAN settings, but the running controller and actual client connections remain the more useful checks.

RadSec on Wi-Fi and the wired switch

Both UniFi APs and Omada now use RadSec with TLS 1.3 to the OPNsense gateway. I kept end-device EAP-TLS separate from that transport. With Omada, my test CA chains failed even when OpenSSL could verify them, so I used a trusted Let's Encrypt chain for the proxy's server identity and kept the separate existing CA for the switch's client identity.

Omada's server-certificate verification is enabled, and I checked successful TLS, new authorization and Accounting. Both UniFi APs were tested as well. The gateway's ACME restart action works; automatic replacement of the proxy's exported PEM at the first real renewal is still an open check. What I tried and what worked →

Watching and resetting one Wi-Fi session

I added RADIUS Accounting to the UniFi enterprise Wi-Fi setup, then checked that the existing FreeRADIUS database recorded Wi-Fi session updates. A Disconnect test made me pay attention to roaming: a session identifier can stop being current while the phone is still on the network. With a fresh session record, the access point acknowledged the targeted Disconnect and the client authenticated again with EAP-TLS.

This is a tested disconnect, not a successful live VLAN change. A later CoA VLAN request returned an Unsupported-Attribute NAK in the tested firmware. The separate IPv6-only test SSID is a different Wi-Fi configuration and hasn't replaced the enterprise SSID.

Omada wired Disconnect: an ACK wasn't enough

I added individual wired 802.1X Disconnect for the configured Omada switch. The first packet received a NAK, but a more selective request produced an authenticated ACK. Then I found that Omada sometimes left the old Accounting row open after the client returned.

I made cleanup wait for a uniquely matching successor Accounting-Start on the same client and port. The final tests passed on two physical ports, with a workstation and a domain controller. There is no arbitrary port shutdown or automatic retry on uncertain outcomes. The Accounting incident → · RADIUS Control →
